# lens-admissions

This lens describes how weekly hospital admissions reach the archive. A week's count is first published in the issue after the week and grows in later issues as late reports arrive, so the most recent weeks always understate what will eventually be reported. The lens estimates, per jurisdiction, the share of the eventual count visible at each reporting lag by the chain ladder: the ratio of what the same weeks showed in consecutive issues, chained back from a lag of 8 weeks, beyond which a week is taken as complete. Dividing the latest vintage by those shares gives the nowcast that theories are fitted to.

Around a theory's expected count, the lens assumes negative binomial observation noise, the standard model for overdispersed surveillance counts: reporting, testing and admission practice add variation beyond Poisson. Its size is estimated by the method of moments from a theory's residuals and is never fitted below 1, so the noise model stays a correction to the theory rather than a substitute for it. It is a library: theories import it so that the same reporting model sits under every one of them, and a matched pair differs only in its mechanism.
