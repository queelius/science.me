# lens-ed

This lens describes how weekly emergency-department visits reach the archive. It has the shape of lens-admissions (a chain-ladder reporting curve, negative binomial noise, the same pmf builder) but its reporting and noise models are fitted to what the ED channel shows in the w-alpha archive through issue 10.

Reporting. ED visits arrive fast. A week's count is first published in the issue after the week at about 83% of what it will eventually show, reaches about 97.5% in the next issue and does not change after its third report; admissions in the same world take four or more issues to complete (about 50%, 77%, 91% and 97%). The curve was the same to three decimals in the two jurisdictions with visits in the hundreds (0.828 and 0.975 in j0, 0.827 and 0.976 in j1), while the two with a handful of visits a week cannot show it at all: once rounded, one or two visits are never revised, and a curve fitted to j2 alone puts the first report at 89%, to j3 alone at 100%. So the lens fits one curve per stream, the chain ladder over the summed values of every jurisdiction, and applies it to each. Revisions are followed for four issues after a week's first report, and a week older than that is taken as complete; a target resolves four issues after its week, so that is all a forecast needs. The curve is re-estimated from each stream's own vintages at every origin, so an ED stream that reports differently gets its own (w-beta's first four issues give about 78% and then 97%).

Noise. ED counts are noisier than their size would suggest. Around a smooth weekly curve fitted by maximum likelihood to j0 and j1 together, the negative binomial size is 20 to 40, and 12 to 21 once corrected for the curve's own parameters; the lens takes 15. A theory's residuals give a far smaller noise: seir-core fitted to ten weeks follows the counts closely enough that the method of moments around it gives sizes of 140 to 3000. So the lens uses the method-of-moments size of the theory's residuals, as lens-admissions does, but never above 15: the residuals may say the noise is wider than the archive shows, never narrower. The size is never fitted below 1, so the noise model stays a correction to the theory rather than a substitute for it. Every forecast it builds is cut where its upper tail holds less than one millionth of the mass, and what is cut is left to the uniform floor that scoring mixes into every forecast.

Known weaknesses. The noise floor comes from two jurisdictions over nine weeks, so it is uncertain (around a quadratic curve the 95% profile interval of the size runs from about 7 to 70), and it is w-alpha's: a stream whose counts are noisier is followed by the residuals, but one that is quieter keeps the floor. One curve per stream is wrong where jurisdictions report on different schedules. The lens does not repair seir-core's under-dispersion: on simulated ED-like epidemics with this noise, the 90% intervals of a theory through lens-ed held about 58% of the outcomes, against 55% through lens-admissions (and 56% against 53% when the simulated noise was quieter, size 40, so even there the floor helped). It is a library: theories import it so that the same reporting model sits under every one of them, and a matched pair differs only in its mechanism.
